NHS GP Prescribing of Sleep Medications: Temazepam (2020–2025) Using OpenPrescribing

(November 2025)

OpenPrescribing

Temazepam is a benzodiazepine licenced in the UK for short term use in severe insomnia in adults. In “Trend for BNF sections”, chapter 4 (Central Nervous System), under 4.1 Hypnotics and anxiolytics, the prescribing items and spending for each drug across all practices in England can be explored individually. For temazepam, a notable discrepancy emerges, as prescription volume does not align well with spending, with some time periods showing the two trends moving in opposite directions (Figure 1). The contrasting pattern suggests that spending is not directly driven by the number of prescriptions (items), but more likely by differences in the quantity prescribed and changes in the actual cost of Temazepam over time. It also implies that periods with fewer prescriptions may have involved larger quantities per prescription or higher-cost formulations. Further investigation is needed to better understand these dynamics and uncover the story behind the trends.

Additional examination of the downloaded prescribing data for Temazepam is presented below (Table, Figure 2): Between September 2020 and May 2022, prescription items (mean: 60k, SD: 4.1k), quantity (1,571k, 119k), and spending (£145k, 20k) across England showed a gradual decline. Beginning in June 2022, items and quantity dropped sharply, reaching their lowest levels in December 2022 (items: 16k; quantity: 463k). They then increased and remained relatively stable until August 2025, with average values of 27k items and 721k quantity for the period January 2023 to August 2025. Spending, however, followed a different pattern, rising dramatically from June 2022 to a peak of £1,112k in September before declining through December, fluctuating briefly, and then stabilising from March 2023 until August 2025. The period from June 2022 to February 2023 is particularly notable: while prescribing items and quantity fell sharply, spending increased significantly (costs more than tripled) - an interesting divergence in trends that warrants further investigation.
